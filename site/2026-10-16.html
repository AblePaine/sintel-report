No new frontier model landed today. Instead, the layer above the models hardened into a market in 48 hours: ServiceNow shipped a cryptographic passport for agents, Workday opened HR and finance headless to any MCP agent, Radware shipped MCP-server protection, Pluto raised $42M to police vibe-coded tools, and Salt Security put a number on the failure mode — 49.8% of organisations say an agent already took an unintended or unauthorised action. Agent governance — the category formed this week, with receipts#

  • ServiceNow launched AI Passport (Oct 15) — cryptographically signed credentials an agent presents at runtime to prove its authorised scope, covering first- and third-party agents and MCP servers, with graduated enforcement up to a machine-speed kill switch, NVIDIA as design and launch partner, and expected availability in Q4 2026. The expanded Control Tower adds shadow-AI detection and 400+ discovery connectors. Futurum ServiceNow via HPCwire Edge: the credential, not the policy document, is pitched as the governance layer. That is an identity claim — whoever issues the passport becomes the toll booth every enterprise agent must pass.
  • Salt Security quantified why the category exists — in its State of Agentic AI and API Security 2H 2026 report (survey of 380+ professionals plus production telemetry), 49.8% confirmed or suspected an agent took an unintended, unexpected, or unauthorised action in the past year; among them, over half involved an unauthorised external API call or transaction and over 40% involved data modification or deletion. Only 12.5% could consistently trace an agent's full path from instruction to outcome, and only 17.5% could automatically halt a misbehaving agent. 86.4% already run at least one agent; 76.8% run agents that chain into other agents and tools. Salt Security Edge: read the methodology honestly — confirmed-or-suspected is not a verified breach rate. The traceability numbers are the harder signal: deployment is at 86%, end-to-end observability is at 12.5%. That gap is the market.
  • Pluto Security emerged from stealth with $42M ($8.5M seed led by TLV Partners, $34M Series A led by Jump Capital, announced Oct 13) — a platform that maps agents across endpoints, cloud, and SaaS, inventories the models, tools, skills, and MCP servers behind them, and can intervene mid-run. Pluto reports 40+ enterprise customers, 1M+ secured endpoints, and over $2M ARR, and says roughly 60% of detected risks come from tools built by non-developers — finance first, then marketing and legal, with professional developers fourth. SiliconANGLE Edge: the risk source flipped. The perimeter problem is no longer the engineering org shipping agents; it is every other department vibe-coding internal tools with live data access. Governance demand is coming from below, not from the platform team.
  • Radware shipped MCP Server Protection (Oct 14) — purpose-built defence for MCP servers themselves against protocol-specific attacks, tool enumeration, DDoS, and abuse, across cloud, Kubernetes, and on-prem deployments, citing a Stacklok survey in which nearly half of software companies report MCP in production. GlobeNewswire Edge: when an incumbent security vendor ships a SKU named for the protocol, the protocol has won the integration layer. MCP's attack surface is now a line item, which is what standardisation looks like from the security side.

Edge signal of the day#

The models stopped being the bottleneck this week; permission became the product. In 48 hours the enterprise stack acquired a passport (ServiceNow), a headless back door with the rules attached (Workday), a protocol firewall (Radware), a $42M-funded police force for employee-built tools (Pluto), and a survey showing deployment at 86% against traceability at 12.5% and automatic containment at 17.5% (Salt). Simultaneously, the consumer side got a vault that lets an agent pay without ever seeing a card. Every one of those products exists because agents are already acting — on pay data, on lab instruments, on live APIs — faster than anyone built the authority layer. The labs spent two years racing on capability; the money this week moved to the layer that decides what capability is allowed to do, who issued that permission, and what receipt proves it. That layer is unconsolidated, standards are days old, and the small-fleet tier has no vendor at all. That is where the edge moved today.
