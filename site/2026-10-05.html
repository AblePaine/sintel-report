Past 24h#

  • Beam is the first US open-weight answer to the Chinese open models. Nvidia-backed Reflection (ex-DeepMind founders, ~$25B valuation) unveiled Beam on Oct 5: text-only MoE, 501B total params with 23B active per task, pretrained on 23.8T tokens, 1M-token context. Weights land later this month under Apache 2.0, with a technical report and model card; waitlist open while final red-teaming runs. Reflection claims comparable advanced-reasoning to Z.ai's GLM-5.2 at roughly 1/4–1/3 the inference compute — company-reported, not independently verified, and its own table shows GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3 ahead on most reported tests. The pitch is "AI factories": enterprises and sovereigns building local AI on their own data — Shinsegae (Korea) already signed a 250MW MOU. The compute war chest behind it is the over-financing leg in live numbers: $150M/month of SpaceX Colossus 2 GB300 time from July 2026 through 2029, plus a $1B+ Nebius deal. reuters · techcrunch · startupfortune analysis
  • Instinct put its agent in group chats — no account required for friends. Early-access users can now add the Instinct agent to chats with friends; the group agent works even if the friends haven't joined Instinct. Founder Noah Shinn spelled out the trust model: the personal agent asks permission before connecting to the group's agent, and users can remove trust per group. This moves Instinct ahead of Muse on this feature (Muse still lacks it; Meta AI can sit in WhatsApp/Messenger/IG/FB groups). Context of the week: both added phone calls in the last couple weeks, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Dots in Spaces last week. techcrunch
  • Snaplii shipped a separate wallet for AI-agent spending. The digital wallet app launched Snaplii Cash: a pre-funded agent wallet with a user-set spending limit (e.g. $50) plus cashback — the agent spends from the wallet, and credit-card/bank details are never shared with it. The release pairs with the PYMNTS finding (69% of consumers interested in delegating tasks) and Visa research (only 23% trust generative AI with payments) — the budget-capped wallet is the trust bridge between the two. newswire
  • Agent payments hit scale: ~12M x402 payments on the XRP Ledger. Tracked x402 payments went from 1M to nearly 12M in under three months (525K/day average), with AI agents buying mostly data and AI inference, denominated in XRP or RLUSD. RippleX engineering's pitch: 3–5s settlement, predictable fees; the t54 framework adds budgets, merchant restrictions, and approval requirements for delegated agent purchases. Adjacent, same week: the Ethereum Foundation launched zkAPI — a one-time ETH/USDC deposit becomes a private note spent via zero-knowledge proof, with a local client speaking OpenAI/Ollama endpoints at localhost, so the payment server sees a valid payment but never who paid. news.bitcoin.com
  • JarvisLabs hiked H100 on-demand rates +29.7% to $3.49/hr, effective today. H200 $3.99 → $4.59, RTX PRO 6000 $1.89 → $2.19. The provider's stated reason: "a global shortage of GPU memory (HBM3e and HBM3 on the H200 and H100, GDDR7 on the RTX PRO 6000)." Existing committed nodes keep their contract rate. This is the counter-canary to the falling-rent read: rents are rising exactly where memory is the binding constraint — capacity exists, memory doesn't — which is the over-financing/duration-mismatch framing, not the physical-glut one. jarvislabs provider page
  • Cohere shipped North 2 — the locked-down enterprise answer, same day as Beam. Redesigned agent harness, cross-session agent memory, model-agnostic operation, and full deployment control across cloud, on-premises, and air-gapped — plus North Admin with granular cost controls, rate limits, and user quotas. The year's production deployments across finance/health/telecom/energy shaped it. The day's shape is clear: the open answer (Beam) and the locked-down answer (North 2) landed within hours of each other. unite.ai
  • Horowitz: "the infrastructure for AI is wrong." In the Vintage x Vintage EP1 clip with James Norman, a16z's Ben Horowitz parallels the early cloud era — everyone thought they could put existing software "on the other side of the wire" and it didn't work; the whole stack got rebuilt. His points: an AI query "could run for a week" and the systems to manage that don't exist; NVIDIA's chip is "power-hungry, low-yield... built for playing video games"; training will change "from the model on down." His five-line map: AI is a new computer bigger than the internet; pockets of excess exist but the 1999 math doesn't apply; money can now buy a decade's lead; customers can rebuild their own software; the infrastructure gets rebuilt. The tension worth naming: a16z just launched a dedicated Machine Age Fund to invest in the infrastructure buildout (chips, memory, networking, power, cooling) — the fund is a bet on the rebuild, not on the current stack. clip (X) · transcript · writeup
