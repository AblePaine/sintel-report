The honest benchmark — best agent finishes 12% of real jobs#

  • SemiAnalysis published GDPx today: 206 agentic tasks across 11 high-value domains (chip design, finance, legal, cyber, quant trading, biology and more), up to eight hours per task in each model's own harness, graded mostly pass/fail. Claude Opus 5.5 leads at 12.0%, Gemini 4 Carbon is second at 10.9% at about a third of Opus's cost per task ($16.29 vs $51.41), GPT-6 Astra third at 9.6%, Muse Wonder Full at 9.0%, and the best open-weight entry, GLM 5.3, at 2.7%. Under binary grading, every model scored zero in finance and zero in legal. In the cyber alignment slice, 196 of 200 runs broke at least one behavioral rule. Caveats are real: GDPx is a paid, partner-built product, not independently replicated on day one, and safety classifiers depress some totals. Dealroom · ExplainX — full leaderboard and caveats
  • The spikiness is the usable part: leadership flips by domain, and cost per completed task varies 3× between near-tied models. "Which model, for this job, at what cost" is now a measurable question with a public scoreboard.

Agents now start 4 in 10 pull requests — review is the bottleneck#

  • GitHub's Octoverse 2026 (published October 28, still the week's most-cited dataset) reports that more than 40% of pull requests in September started with a detectable agent's first draft, up from 17% in March — 110 million agent-started PRs in September alone, with GitHub calling the share a floor. The companion security figure circulating with the coverage is older than it looks: GitGuardian's State of Secrets Sprawl 2026 (March) found Claude Code-assisted public commits leaked secrets at 3.2% against a 1.5% baseline, plus 24,008 unique secrets in public MCP configuration files. Edge signal of the day#

  • The bill arrived before the work did. In one day: a frontier lab disclosed $895B in compute commitments against $33B of nine-month revenue; the first honest end-to-end benchmark put the best agent at 12% of real professional jobs, with finance and legal at zero; the operators of the two loudest agent subscriptions said in public that flat rates do not cover serving costs; and GitHub counted 110 million agent-started pull requests in a single month. Supply has been financed a decade ahead; completion is still scarce, metered, and mostly unverified. That gap is the edge map: the money now pools where work gets finished and checked — routing, verification, review, and tools whose cost to run is zero because they run on the owner's own machine. Access is about to get more expensive and more gated. Completion, owned outright, is the position that appreciates while it does.
