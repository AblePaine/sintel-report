Tencent shipped the Hy4 weights — 770B open flagship, custom licence, $50M trigger#

  • Tencent published the full Hy4 weights today on Hugging Face, ModelScope and GitCode: a 770B-parameter MoE with 49B active per token, native vision and computer use, a 1,048,576-token context, BF16 at roughly 1.55 TB and an FP8 build at roughly 770 GB, served via vLLM and SGLang, with the system card covering safety, cyber and alignment evals. The licence is the story. Hy4 ships under the Tencent Hy License, derived from Apache 2.0 but not Apache 2.0: section 10 requires a separate written licence from model-as-a-service operators above US$50 million in revenue or 100 million monthly users; purely internal use is exempt. Tencent's own benchmarks put Hy4 mid-pack against closed rivals — first on none of its 12 headline tests — so the release's weight is structural, not leaderboard: a downloadable flagship with a meter built into the licence. The open/closed line now runs through the licence text, tier by tier, exactly as the Qwen4-Max reversal signalled earlier this week. Banking watch: the liability transfer went industry-wide this week — Wells Fargo's Section 15 takes effect Tuesday#

  • Routine-grade on its own, convergent in aggregate: TechCrunch reported this week, per a clause-by-clause reading published yesterday, that Citi changed its terms to the same effect, PNC changed its terms the same day, and Citizens emailed customers a similar warning — joining JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America, which took the same line last month. Wells Fargo's version is the template: Section 15 of its updated Online Access Agreement, effective Tuesday, November 24, defines an AI agent broadly, deems any agent-initiated instruction to have been initiated and authorised directly by the customer, states in capitals that agent use is "AT YOUR OWN RISK," expressly lists losses from "incomplete, inaccurate, or manipulated inputs" — the contract name for prompt injection — and puts the burden of proving revocation on the customer. Four banks chose hard blocks instead; the shared answer across the largest US banks, arrived at in about five weeks, is that whoever lets an agent in carries what the agent does. No aggregator breach, no new block, no delegated-authorisation standard launched today — the PlotMyCash callout in the brief below is for the convergence and the Tuesday effective date, not a trigger event. Edge signal#

Agents got identities, distribution and a bill — all in one week. Muse handed every user an agent with its own emailable address and revealed 200,000 silent browser installs; OpenAI pushed always-on agents down to the $20 tier; Gemini made carrying hundreds of tools nearly free. And in the same window, the largest banks in America finished writing the other half of the contract: anything an agent does in an account is the customer's own act, prompt-injection losses included, effective in Wells Fargo's case on Tuesday. Capability is compounding on schedule — a 770B flagship is downloadable, a frontier-class model runs on a laptop, ANT trades on Nasdaq. What became scarce this week is not intelligence or access; it is accountable identity — an agent that can be named, reached, authorised, audited and billed, with a revocation that can be proved. Every toll booth of the next phase sits on that layer, and as of today it is the one layer nobody has finished building.
