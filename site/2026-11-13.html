Past 24h overview#

Open weights just became an audit trail: six Chinese models carry a Blackwell fingerprint#

  • Former OpenAI researcher Will DePue, publishing through Unemployment Labs on November 12, reports that the published weights of six models — DeepSeek-V4-Flash and V4-Pro, Moonshot's Kimi K3 and K3.1-Pro, MiniMax-M3 and Ant Group's Ling-3.1-flash — carry the numerical fingerprint of NVIDIA Blackwell hardware, which cannot be exported to China without a licence. The method reads the streaming-multiprocessor count left in a model's starting weights (148 for B200/B300, 152 for GB200/GB300), searched roughly six trillion seed-and-layout combinations, and agreed with all 24 models whose makers have disclosed their hardware. Earlier models from the same four labs read as Hopper; the newest Qwen, Zhipu, Tencent, Xiaomi and StepFun models still do. The limits matter as much as the finding: the fingerprint identifies the hardware that generated the starting weights, not necessarily every chip that trained the model, and it says nothing about where the chips were or who owned them — Blackwell capacity rented outside China leaves the same trace, so the report does not establish smuggling or an export-control breach. DePue's X post passed 5.8 million views, the highest-engagement technical story in the window. The structural point survives every caveat: openness is now auditable. The more completely a lab publishes weights, the more completely outsiders can reconstruct its supply chain. The Register · MadRobot

Schwab opens the brokerage to agents — its own MCP server, client approval on every trade#

  • Charles Schwab announced on November 12 its own Model Context Protocol server, letting clients connect an LLM — starting with Anthropic's Claude — directly to Schwab accounts. Schwab reports $13.33 trillion in total client assets and 10.7 million daily average trades in Q3 2026. The pilot is limited to a select group of Claude users, retail only — registered investment advisers are excluded for now — with wider access and additional AI platforms promised over the coming months. The control design is the story: the agent can access account data, review portfolios, set alerts and, on thinkorswim-enabled accounts, prepare trades, but the client personally confirms every trade. Coverage that described this as agents gaining direct execution rights overstates the launch. Following a September deal that made Anthropic Schwab's exclusive custody AI partner, the vendor-neutral protocol also gives Schwab leverage to add competing models later — RIABiz notes the exclusivity is unlikely to survive the architecture. Decision models go open and near-free: Perplexity Decider v1.3 undercuts the paid decision APIs in 48 hours#

  • Perplexity released Decider v1.3 and Decider Flash v1.3 on November 12 under Apache 2.0: decision models that return probabilities for yes/no, choice and scoring questions instead of generating text — 27B built on Qwen3.8-27B and a 4B Flash distilled from it. The new capability is Locate, which returns verbatim text spans with character offsets alongside the decision, aimed at extraction, entity and PII work. Self-hosting splits by size: the 27B needs roughly 49 GiB of weights, Flash roughly 8.5 GiB, within reach of a single high-memory local GPU. Edge signal#

Verification ate another layer — weights, trades and tool contracts all became readable in one day. A forensic method now reads a model's birth hardware out of its open weights and validates 24 for 24. A $13.33 trillion custodian decided agent access to brokerage accounts is shippable only with a human confirmation on every trade. Decision models that return probabilities — and, with Locate, their own evidence spans — went open-source at a cent per million tokens. None of these stories is about a model getting smarter. All of them are about a claim becoming checkable: where a model was born, whether a trade was approved, why a router chose, what a tool contract said yesterday versus today. Generation kept getting cheaper in the same window, as it does every window. The pricing power, the regulatory attention and the enterprise anxiety are all migrating to the proof layer — provenance, approval and audit that a third party can verify without trusting the vendor. Builds that produce that proof as a by-product, held by the owner and portable across platforms, are compounding. Builds that ask to be trusted are now competing with a fingerprint, a confirmation tap and a $0.01 decision that shows its work.
