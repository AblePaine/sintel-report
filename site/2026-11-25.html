Edge signal#

The wait-spiral went hyperscale: the biggest buyer in the market is now refusing to commit, and prices are falling at every tier at once. In October the pattern was retail — full Micro Center shelves, a phantom $17,999 anchor, and buyers who kept waiting until the cuts came. Today the same script ran at the top of the market. Anthropic, by every account, needs more compute, not less — yet it is declining to lock in a 50,000-GPU renewal at current prices while spot falls, and SpaceX is remarketing the block and cutting small-cluster Blackwell quotes by a quarter to move capacity. The transacted index agrees: H100 settles down ~9% in a week after five weeks of rises, B200 down ~6%. At the retail end, the flagship workstation card took another $500 cut dressed as a holiday promo, and the market's most motivated watcher publicly said it was not enough and kept waiting. When the anchor tenant and the deal-hunter make the same decision in the same week — defer, because next month is cheaper — the deflation stops being a pricing story and becomes a behavior story, and behavior is what makes spirals self-sustaining. The honest limit: list prices have not moved, Dell just booked a $132.3B backlog, and two weeks of falling settles is a crack, not the a16z criterion's "steady decline." December's settles are the verdict. Until then, every long-term compute commitment — contract, card, or rig — should be priced as if the seller needs the deal more than the buyer does, because at both ends of the market, that is now demonstrably true.
