Past 24h overview#

Claude now reads bank accounts: Anthropic launches Personal Finance via Plaid#

  • Anthropic launched Personal Finance in Claude on November 5, in US beta for Pro, Max and Ultra subscribers, rolling out in waves. Accounts link through Plaid — bank, card, loan and investment accounts, up to 16 banks — and Claude answers spending, balance and upcoming-payment questions in plain language. Access is read-only: Anthropic states Claude cannot move, manage or invest money, the feature is not designed to give investment advice, and Personal Finance chats are not used for training. The scope behind the restraint is large: for linked accounts Claude can read balances, credit limits and up to two years of transactions, and linked data can surface in ordinary chats unless Plaid is turned off for chats. Unlinking stops new reads; it does not erase information already used in conversations. The category is now validated by a frontier lab, and the validation runs entirely on an aggregator, in the cloud, on a subscription. Unite.AI · CellCog

Claude Code put signed-in account emails into web requests — and the fix covers one spelling of the leak#

  • A GitHub report filed November 5 (issue #107757, on v2.1.318) says Claude Code placed the signed-in account email address in the User-Agent header of web requests to third-party sites; the reporter links the exposure to a flood of scam mail, a link that rests on the reporter's own account. Anthropic staff acknowledged the mechanism on Hacker News and GitHub: the account email sits in model context, originally to stop invented author names in author tags, and the model sometimes copied it into scripted requests when a site asked for a contact address. Anthropic shipped a built-in mod, cc-plugin-privacy, in v2.1.317 and later, on by default, and says the email leaves model context in a release expected early next week. Independent testing published November 6 found the mod narrow by design: it checks shell, Monitor and PowerShell calls for the literal address next to a User-Agent reference, its own README calls it "a courtesy, not a boundary," and WebFetch, WebSearch and MCP paths are not covered; one test run found 8 of 23 leak shapes passing the hook. The stable channel was still on 2.1.311 in November 6 reporting, without the guard. A separate July issue (#81138) documenting the email in the system prompt remains open. MadRobot · Tin

GPT-6 Cyber is named, priced and Critical-gated — while an open model matched the shape for about $1,000 a target#

  • OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber on November 5: a cyber-specialised variant of GPT-6 Astra, rated Critical for cybersecurity under the Preparedness Framework — the first cyber model at that tier — and available only through the API and Codex to approved Daybreak Red customers with a separate model approval, at $20 input / $100 output per million tokens. OpenAI reports 48.5% on ExploitGym against 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber, the 948 working Linux privilege-escalation exploits and 1,724 reproduced vulnerabilities from the November 4 kernel study, and 81 previously unknown Firefox vulnerabilities Mozilla rated high severity. All figures are OpenAI-reported. The open-side counterpoint, recirculating in November 5-6 roundups, is Epoch AI's October 29 study: the downloadable GLM-5.3 built working one-click Firefox exploits against six consecutive releases from published-but-unshipped patches, at a marginal cost of about $1,000 per version after a harness that cost more than $100,000 to build, with no provider refusals reported; the same effort against Chrome produced no equivalent result. The gated frontier and the ungated open model are not identical — scope, support and safeguards differ — but on browser exploitation the capability gap is now measured in access paperwork, not in kind. MLQ · Silicon Noon

METR: Anthropic's model progress is running at about twice the old rate — and most of the evidence is redacted#

  • METR published its high-access assessment of Anthropic on November 5 after an 11-week evaluation (August 12 to October 29). Its headline estimate: Anthropic is improving model capabilities on well-specified tasks at about twice the March 2024 to October 2025 rate — 1.9x on the internal AECI benchmark index, with a 95% interval of 1.5x to 2.8x and a stated plausible range of 1.3x to 3x — and a majority, perhaps about 80%, of the acceleration is attributable to AI used in the research itself, not to faster compute scaling. METR also says Anthropic's August Risk Report understates the acceleration, and that crossing the Responsible Scaling Policy's 2x acceleration threshold by mid-July was plausible, which Anthropic disputes. The evidential limit is stated by METR itself: Anthropic held unilateral redaction rights and redacted a majority of the evidence METR found most informative. The acceleration claim therefore rests substantially on an evaluator's judgment that the public cannot inspect — published the same week the company asks public investors to price it. AWS turns the cited web answer into a one-cent MCP call#

  • AWS made Web Answer on Amazon Bedrock AgentCore generally available on November 5: one MCP tool call returns a synthesised, cited answer to a natural-language question, priced at $10 per 1,000 calls in us-east-1 only, at a 10-transactions-per-second account limit, with domain include/deny controls. Web Search on the same platform stays at $7 per 1,000 queries without the synthesis. Search, fetch, synthesis and citation — a pipeline agents previously assembled from parts — is now a managed commodity with a published per-call price, which reprices every hand-built search-RAG stack and every content business whose value was the synthesis step rather than the underlying data. Edge signal of the day#

  • Custody became the scarce asset today, and every headline priced it. A frontier lab asked subscribers to hand two years of bank transactions to an aggregator-mediated cloud context, in the same 24 hours its coding agent was shown leaking account emails to arbitrary websites, its outside evaluator published a doubling of progress with the evidence redacted, and its IPO price fell 40% from October talk while revenue hit a record. None of those stories is about capability — capability is abundant, downloadable at $1,000 a browser exploit on the open side and gated at $100 per million tokens on the frontier side. Each story is about who holds the data, the identity, the evidence and the asset when the work is done. The positions that compound remain the ones where custody is structural: weights on owned metal, finance data that never leaves the machine, engines that compute rather than summarise, and records kept daily so the pattern is provable later. The market spent today discovering the price of renting all four. The portfolio already owns them.
