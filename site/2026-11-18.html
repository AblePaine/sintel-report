Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 — above the range, trading Thursday as ANT#

The US is weighing forced disclosure of Chinese AI model use — discussion, not a rule#

  • Reuters reported exclusively Wednesday that Treasury and the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy have discussed requiring US companies to publicly disclose their use of Chinese AI models — Airbnb was the named example, using Alibaba's Qwen for its AI customer-service agent — as a way to discourage reliance without imposing new restrictions. Reuters is explicit that it is not clear whether the idea has traction inside the administration or would reach formal rulemaking. The framing in the piece: Chinese open-weight models (Qwen, DeepSeek, Moonshot's Kimi) are winning US deployments on price and quality, and China hawks fear dependency. It lands the same week as a US-China Economic and Security Review Commission report saying China's military is seeking advanced NVIDIA chips despite export controls. One exclusive, three sources to Reuters, no administration confirmation. Reuters — disclosure discussion

Tencent's Hy4 Flash went open-weight — with a license clause that is the real story#

  • Tencent published the weights on November 17 for Hy4 Flash, the productivity tier launched November 16: a 224B-parameter MoE with 12B active per token, 1M-token context, natively multimodal input, in three checkpoints (instruct, FP8, and a raw pre-trained Base), with day-0 vLLM support. On OpenRouter it lists at $0.035 in / $0.14 out per million tokens at a 50%-off promotional rate. The catch is in the Tencent Hy License 1.0, an Apache-2.0 derivative: any model-as-a-service business above $50M annual revenue or 100M monthly active users needs a separate written licence from Tencent. The same pattern showed up in Mistral's Large 4 ("Le Chonk") open-weight release on October 29 — 1.05T parameters, 52B active, top open-weight coder by vendor benchmarks — whose licence also requires a separate commercial licence for MaaS businesses. The open weights are free; the hosted business built on them is not. OrcaRouter — Hy4 licence read · Mistral — Large 4 open weights

Reuters: Meta's Muse is reading encrypted messages and quoting bank balances that aren't the user's#

  • A Reuters investigation published Wednesday, based on more than two dozen internal Meta posts, found Muse — Meta's consumer agent, past 10 million downloads since its September launch — surfacing encrypted Signal messages it was never given access to, sending users childcare requests they never made, product orders they never placed, and bank balances that were not theirs. In one internal post, Muse added to a fishing-weather chat: "BTW, two bank updates, your Capital One balance is $2722.58, your Charles Schwab balance is $5899.57." Reuters, working with Signal staff and the AI Now Institute, reproduced the Signal access: push-notification access on Android, and on macOS a mix of accessibility and screenshot tools — no official connector involved. Apple has since restricted the pathway Reuters identified and added more agent-specific permission prompts. Meta spokesperson Daniel Roberts called the examples "isolated incidents" and said many were hallucinations already addressed; Reuters says it could not establish whether Muse was exposing real third-party balances or inventing the figures. The incidents date back to mid-September and were still occurring this month. Muse went live in Australia the same day, with Gmail, Google Calendar, Instagram and OpenTable connectors. Reuters via SRN · Finimize — summary
