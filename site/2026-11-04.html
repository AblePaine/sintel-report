Past 24h overview#

Data centers got crushed at the ballot box#

  • The November 3 midterms put data centers directly on local ballots, and the result was a rout. Roughly 40–45 local measures across 10 states proposed bans, moratoriums, or new restrictions; tracking site Data Centers on the Ballot counted at least 33 passed with six more leading as of Wednesday morning, and only one jurisdiction — Harwood, North Dakota (population under 1,000) — voted a restriction down. Ohio was ground zero: 20–21 measures across 18 municipalities, with restrictions winning or projected in every one, and the mayor and two council members in Piqua, Ohio recalled in part over a Meta-backed project; Yukon's mayor in Oklahoma was likewise recalled. USA Today · Route Fifty · Engadget
  • The sentiment is national, not just local: NBC exit polling found 56% of voters calling AI and data centers a "crucial or major factor" in their congressional vote, and about 76% saying they would oppose a new data center in their community. State-level candidates who ran on guardrails won upsets in Texas, Georgia, and Michigan. Pre-election reporting put at least $100B of proposed projects in the crosshairs, after at least $68B was already halted or blocked in Q2. Some local votes are advisory rather than binding, but the direction is unambiguous: siting permission, not silicon, is now a priced constraint on the buildout. Inman

Kimi K3.1 Pro weights land on schedule — and the license is a toll that only bites at scale#

  • Moonshot published the Kimi K3.1 Pro weights on Hugging Face on November 4, on the promised date: 2.8T total / 104B active MoE, 96 safetensors shards, MXFP4 weights with MXFP8 activations, 1M context, with the cyber-scrubbed checkpoint (internal exploit benchmark 81.0, down from the unfiltered 90.5). Original weights run about 1.56 TB; the recommended serving stack is vLLM, SGLang, or TokenSpeed on data-center hardware — still a cluster release, not a desktop one. CellCog · VRAM Glass
  • The license question that stayed open through yesterday is answered: the Kimi K3.1 Pro License is MIT-style — use, modify, fine-tune, and sell freely — with two conditions. A model-hosting business past $20M in annual revenue needs a separate commercial agreement with Moonshot; a product past 100M monthly users (or $20M monthly revenue) must display the Kimi K3.1-Pro name in its interface. Internal use and certified inference partners are exempt. The pattern is now consistent across the open-weight frontier (Mistral's commercial carve-out, now Kimi's revenue gate): free below scale, tolled at scale. ExplainX

AMD's data-center segment nearly doubles — Helios racks are live at all five hyperscalers#

  • AMD reported Q3 on November 3: record revenue of $13.4B, up 45% year over year, with Data Center revenue a record $8.5B, up 97%, now 64% of total sales, driven by EPYC and the first Instinct MI450 shipments. Claude can now vouch for its users — identity becomes MCP distribution#

  • Anthropic's Claude auto-connect (beta, Team and Enterprise) lets Claude itself issue the signed identity assertion (ID-JAG) that MCP's Enterprise-Managed Authorization extension requires, instead of waiting for an identity provider that supports the extension — at launch, only Okta did. Supabase shipped support on November 3: an admin registers Claude as the issuer once, and every member with a matching SSO identity gets the MCP server with existing role permissions and no per-member consent screen. The security caveat is stated plainly in the docs: matching is by email, with no SSO or MFA check on the Claude side, and registering Claude as issuer displaces the IdP's assertions until it is restored. The structural point stands: whoever issues identity decides which servers are zero-click inside every enterprise org on that client. Supabase

Apple opens Siri AI to every developer — submissions open in the spring#

  • Apple published a Developer Preview on November 3 (iOS/iPadOS/macOS/visionOS 27.3 betas) letting any app expose its own custom actions and content to Siri AI through App Intents — previously limited to Apple's preset schemas — plus "skills," markdown guides that teach Siri multi-step sequences across an app's actions, and Spotlight-indexed content retrieval. No new entitlement is required. The limits are equally clear: development builds and internal TestFlight only, no App Store use until submissions open in spring 2027, final usage policies still unpublished, and no EU testing on iOS/iPadOS/watchOS. This is a prep window, not a launch: the agent shelf on the largest consumer platform just got a dated opening. eWeek · PPC Land

Edge signal of the day#

  • Permission became the scarce asset. In 24 hours, the AI buildout's constraint visibly moved off silicon and onto permission: voters revoked permission to build in more than 30 jurisdictions while demand numbers (AMD +97%, fal at $800M+ annualized revenue) said build faster; the strongest open model in the world shipped with a license that is permission priced by scale — free until a business is large, tolled exactly when it matters; an AI lab became an identity issuer, because permission to connect is now the distribution chokepoint for enterprise agents; and a bank's venture arm paid to lead a $150M round in governing what agents may touch. Chips can be manufactured and models can be downloaded. Permission — to site a data center, to host a model at scale, to let an agent into an account — is granted by voters, licensors, platforms, and counterparties, and every one of those grantors started charging or refusing this week. The positions that compound are the ones that need the least permission: owned metal on already-powered land, open weights under thresholds that leave small operators free, local software that asks no platform for access to a customer's own data. That is the portfolio's existing shape — the day's work is recognizing that the market just started pricing it.
