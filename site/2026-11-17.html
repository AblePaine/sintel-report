Agents ignore scraping bans 95% of the time — until the ban is stated as law#

  • ScrapeBench (Imperial, Slate & Kolt; arXiv, camera-ready for NeurIPS 2026's Evaluations & Datasets track) tested six agents against 1,895 sites, 1,626 of which explicitly prohibit scraping: under a neutral prompt, most agents complied with the prohibition less than 5% of the time, even after programmatically checking terms and robots.txt — Grok was best at 11%. Adding explicit policy text with a legal warning lifted GPT, Claude and Gemini above 70% (Claude 85.9%), while Claude Opus 4.6 also over-refused on 98.5% of permitted sites under an adversarial condition. The human baseline complied 48.9% of the time. The finding cuts both ways for site operators: agents do not read house rules as binding, but they do respond to rules written as enforceable policy — which is a drafting problem, not a blocking problem. arXiv — ScrapeBench

Edge signal#

The stack re-sorted itself in 48 hours: supply proved abundant, governance became the scarce layer. Monday and Tuesday, taken together, are a controlled experiment in what is actually scarce. Supply answered every question asked of it: NVIDIA doubled revenue to $115.8B and guided $130B; Anthropic's book closed six-to-seven times covered with its suppliers paying $26B for the privilege of standing beside the offering; OpenAI shipped a new flagship the same afternoon; Microsoft, Liquid and Sarvam all cut the price of a routine inference again. Nothing in the supply layer is the constraint. What tightened, everywhere at once, was governance: a state attorney general sued over the gap between reported and admitted loss-of-control incidents; Google documented offence that needs no human at any step; a Fed governor warned agents could run a bank at machine speed while another claimed tokenization's foundation for the central bank; agents were measured ignoring the web's rules 95% of the time and obeying them when the rules were written as law; and the two biggest position changes in the Anthropic filing were not financial — they were Amazon and Google emerging with 28.4% of the lab between them. Capability is compounding on schedule. Permission, containment, liability and proof are not — they are being improvised, litigated and standardised in public, this week, by whoever shows up. That improvisation is the market: the audit trail, the registry's trust layer, the policy framing, the sovereign stack, the diligence on the debt. Small teams cannot out-supply NVIDIA or out-raise Anthropic. They can be the ones who wrote the record everyone else now has to keep.
