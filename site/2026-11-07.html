Past 24h overview#

Banks move from blocking agents to billing the customer for them#

  • Reporting consolidated this week shows JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Citi, Morgan Stanley, Wells Fargo and Charles Schwab restricting third-party AI agents that log in with customer credentials, while approved read-only data sharing through aggregators continues. JPMorgan chief executive Jamie Dimon stated the boundary on the third-quarter call: outside agents will not be allowed inside the bank because of the risk of rogue agents. The newer step is contractual: Wells Fargo's amended terms surfaced in reporting on November 2 and take effect November 24, assigning loss to the customer who delegates access, and the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency published a nonbinding examiner resource on November 4. Identity vendor Socure's Agent Intelligence suite — agent detection generally available, a trust score in beta, and a verifiable agent credential planned for 2027 — is the commercial version of the same answer: detection first, scoped authorization second. No aggregator breach or credential leak was reported in this window. American Banker via Pivot News · Biometric Update · Agent liability analysis

The three frontier labs confirm weeks of joint safety coordination#

  • OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind confirmed this week that they spent several weeks coordinating on cyber-focused models, safeguards and access programs, after years of mostly competing in public. Separate reporting on the longer-running talks describes shared technical evaluations, pre-release audits and a proposed industry standards body modelled on financial self-regulation, first proposed in July. Details on whether the coordination continues past the cyber theme were not disclosed, and the strongest current reporting rests on a small number of secondary sources, so the fact of coordination is firmer than its institutional shape. The structural read is the gatekeeper cycle in plain form: the three largest labs writing the safety rules that smaller rivals and open models would then have to meet. BitRss · BBN Times

Meta buys Conductor, and the standalone product gets a shutdown clock#

  • Meta acquired Conductor on November 4, confirmed by Meta to TechCrunch and by founder Charlie Holtz the same day; financial terms were not disclosed. Conductor builds no models — it runs Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and other coding agents in parallel isolated workspaces — and the team joins Meta Superintelligence Labs to work on a new interface promised for early 2027. New sign-ups and downloads stopped November 4, Conductor Cloud becomes read-only November 13 and shuts down November 20, when cloud data deletion begins; the local Mac app continues with new models and critical fixes until early 2027. OpenAI agreed to buy Pierre, a code-storage layer for Codex, the same day. The coding-agent race is consolidating around the orchestration layer, and acquired standalone tools are being wound down on a two-week clock. TechRepublic · DEV

Anthropic gives enterprises a veto over Claude before generation#

  • Anthropic's Claude Enterprise security update, announced November 5, makes inference hooks generally available: prompts and pre-tool-call requests are sent to a customer-hosted data-loss-prevention endpoint — Netskope, Palo Alto Networks, Proofpoint, Zscaler, or an internal server — and Claude proceeds only on an allow verdict. The default timeout is five seconds, verdicts are binary with no rewriting, and file and image bytes are excluded from inspection. A Security Center comparing chat, Cowork and Claude Code settings against recommended configuration, a parent admin console, and endpoint sensors inside the Cowork virtual machine remain in beta. It is the enterprise twin of the bank move: agent use continues, but only through an authorization gate the institution controls. AI Weekly · CIO coverage

  • Catherine "Cat" Wu, Anthropic's head of product for Claude Code, announced on November 5 that she is joining OpenAI to lead product management for Core Products, reporting to Thibault Sottiaux and working with the enterprise and consumer product leads. Her last day at Anthropic was October 30. OpenAI confirmed the hire to Business Insider. The move lands weeks before Anthropic's planned public listing and underlines that the coding-agent contest is now a product and talent contest as much as a model contest. OfficeChai · WinBuzzer

Open weights: a crowded week, with two headline releases still promises#

  • Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Small on November 5 — 594B total parameters, 17B active, image input, score of 31 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — live on its Tinker platform at $0.30 input / $1.00 output per million tokens, with full weights promised next week under Apache 2.0 after external safety testing. Until those weights post, it is a hosted model with an open-weights date attached. Tencent's full Hy4 followed the same pattern on November 4: 770B mixture-of-experts, 1M context, first Hy model with vision and computer use across command line and graphical interfaces, priced at 6 yuan input / 18 yuan output per million tokens with a 50% TokenHub discount through December 31; the final weights are planned, not released, while the August preview weights are already public. Baidu took the opposite route on November 6, posting ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base — 280B total, 14B active, trained on 33.5 trillion tokens — to Hugging Face as a raw base model with no fine-tuning, leaving post-training to whoever downloads it. Tencent's WeChat Vision team also open-sourced WeLM-Image on November 6, a 31.7B image model designed for single-GPU deployment. Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro, whose weights published November 4, currently tops the open-weights ranking at 55 on the Artificial Analysis index, and llama.cpp merged text-only K3.1 support on November 6 while vLLM support is still partial. Agility Robotics becomes the first public pure-play humanoid#

  • Agility Robotics completed its business combination with Churchill Capital Corp XI on November 4 and began Nasdaq trading as AGLT on November 5, with about $465 million in gross capital before expenses and a pre-money equity valuation of about $2.5 billion. The caution sits in the order book: more than $300 million in multi-year Digit 5 orders is potential value contingent on milestones and specifications, not recognized revenue, with early-access deliveries expected in the first half of 2027. Quarterly filings will now put public numbers on humanoid unit economics for the first time. SEC filing · TechRepublic · DeepTech

Memory supercycle: GPU prices keep climbing, with no end dated#

  • Engadget's November 7 survey of the shortage reports JPMorgan's estimate that DRAM prices will have risen about 400% since 2024 by the end of 2026, with AI data centers projected to consume roughly 70% of global DRAM supply this year and memory makers expecting tight supply through 2027 and 2028. TechSpot's November 2 pricing survey across 10 countries put cards at 44% above launch price on average and as high as 58% in the US and Canada, after cumulative rises of roughly 15% in August, 7% in September and 10% in October; U.S. listings for the RTX 5080 now start near $2,000 against a $999 launch price. The shortage is a memory shortage, and the consumer card is where the bill lands. Engadget · Local AI Master price tracker

Clean-room wave: Craft Anything is gaining real traction#

  • Craft Anything, ArtCraft's clean-room engine in pure Rust, reached version 0.3.0 on November 2 with 8,713 GitHub stars, 1,441 forks, and about 153,000 downloads across the three releases since October 31. The engine studies how a website, app or game behaves, then has a coding agent build an independent version and compares the result against the original; clean-room mode is the default, it exposes more than 300 tools as both CLI commands and MCP tools, and its stated rules refuse DRM, login and anti-cheat bypass. Traction at this level keeps the decompile / clean-room reimplementation wave on the watch list as a landscape shift for legacy software and games, not a one-off demo. Indie Kings

Agent money rails: x402 volume is wide and shallow#

  • The x402 open payment standard — stablecoin payment attached to the HTTP 402 response, no account or sign-up — stood at 197,582,876 cumulative transactions and $42,548,636 in settlement volume as of November 7, with 401,521 transactions on November 6, across 12 chains and 18 facilitators, concentrated on Base. No new institutional framing of the Bitcoin-as-tokenized-compute thesis surfaced today; the live movement is on the spending side, in stablecoins, exactly where the thesis said agent cash flow would run. Sovereign-AI lens, continuing#

  • The sovereign-AI argument circulating this week — enterprises resisting token charges that deliver little value while their workflows and data feed a provider's training pipeline — gained three institutional echoes in 48 hours: banks assigning agent risk back to customers, enterprises inserting their own allow-or-deny hooks in front of a frontier model, and the three largest labs coordinating on shared safety rules. Each is a different industry installing the same layer: a gate between the user and the agent, owned by someone other than the user.

Edge signal#

The authorization layer became the toll booth this week. In 48 hours the pattern repeated across three industries: banks rewrote contracts so delegated agent access carries customer liability (effective November 24), Anthropic shipped an allow-or-deny hook so enterprises can veto a model before it answers, and the three largest labs confirmed joint work on shared safety evaluations and pre-release audits. Agents are not being stopped — they are being permissioned, and permissioned access is access that can be priced, logged, and revoked by whoever owns the gate. The durable positions are the ones that need no gate: local models on owned hardware, offline software with no credentials to delegate, and sites an agent can call without an account. Everything rented through someone else's authorization layer just got a meter attached to it.
