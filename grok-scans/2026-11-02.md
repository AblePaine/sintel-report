## Ways to capitalize
- **The flat-rate AI subscription is cracking in public.** Both Musk and Masad are saying it out loud overnight. That is the opening for AblePaine's buy-once, offline, account-free pitch. Market "no meter, no rug pull" explicitly. Small local models plus a one-time price are now a selling point, not a compromise. Ship one paid-once utility with a visible "runs on your machine, costs you $0/month" promise this month while the backlash is hot.
- **Secondary GPU capacity is a brokerage business.** Funded startups are overcommitted on 3-year B300 contracts while spot slides below contract. A marketplace or escrow that matches stranded reserved capacity to short-term buyers, priced against a public spot index, takes margin from both sides. A weekly "B300 spot vs reserved" tracker is a cheap audience-builder (Radar/GPU tracker material).
- **GDPx gives the map of where agents still fail (12%) and where the money is.** Pick one domain from the benchmark (finance ops, chip-design verification, cyber), build a narrow harness plus a skill pack that beats general models on that slice, and sell it per seat to the people doing that job. The safety-classifier note means "which model will actually do this task" routing is a product in its own right.
- **Vertical skill packs are the new plugin store.** ICS/OT skills from one practitioner show the template: domain tools, agent instructions and tests. Curated, tested, paid skill packs for underserved trades (industrial, legal ops, insurance claims) are a 1997-era "directory plus quality bar" play before the big skill marketplaces lock in.
- **Agent wallets plus MCP equals agents as economic actors.** Kosmo-style "agent signs, server never holds keys" is the right security pattern. The leverage isn't another launchpad. It's the boring rails: spend limits, allowlists and audit trails for agent wallets that a business would actually approve.
- **Retail access to the AI IPO wave goes global.** Japan's $3.5B tranche shows retail demand outside the US. Tools that explain AI-company filings to non-US retail buyers in their own language, covering S-1 numbers, lock-ups and dilution, have a fresh, time-boxed audience.
